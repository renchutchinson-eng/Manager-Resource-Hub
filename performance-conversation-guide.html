MANAGE PERFORMANCE

Performance Conversation Guide

A practical guide to help managers address performance concerns clearly, fairly and constructively.

Before the Conversation

  • Be clear about the specific performance concern.
  • Identify the expected standard or outcome.
  • Prepare specific examples.
  • Consider any relevant context or support already provided.

A Simple Performance Conversation

1. Explain the concern

Describe the performance gap using specific facts and examples.

2. Clarify the expectation

Explain what good performance should look like and why the standard matters.

3. Ask for the employee's perspective

Give the employee an opportunity to explain what may be contributing to the issue.

4. Agree support and actions

Clarify what needs to change, what support is available and what actions each person will take.

5. Set a follow-up

Agree when progress will be reviewed and what improvement should be visible by then.

Example

Instead of:

“Your performance hasn't been good enough.”


Try:

“The expectation is that weekly reports are submitted by Friday at noon. Over the last four weeks, three reports were submitted after the deadline. I’d like to understand what is contributing to the delays.”

Keep the Conversation Focused

  • Address the performance issue, not the person's character.
  • Use facts rather than assumptions.
  • Be clear about the standard expected.
  • Listen before deciding the solution.
  • Document key actions and follow-up.

Quick preparation check

  • What is the performance gap?
  • What standard applies?
  • What examples do I have?
  • What support may be needed?
  • When will we review progress?
