Documentation Quick Guide

A practical guide to help managers create clear, objective and useful records of employee and performance matters.

Why documentation matters

Good documentation creates a clear record of what occurred, what was discussed and what actions were agreed. It supports consistency, follow-through and fair decision-making.

Good documentation is:

  • Factual — record what was observed, said or confirmed.
  • Specific — include relevant dates, events and examples.
  • Objective — avoid assumptions, labels and emotional language.
  • Timely — document matters while the information is still clear.
  • Relevant — capture information connected to the issue being managed.

What should I capture?

  • Date and time of the incident or conversation.
  • The specific behaviour, event or performance concern.
  • The expected standard or requirement.
  • Relevant facts or examples discussed.
  • The employee's response or explanation.
  • Any guidance, support or expectations communicated.
  • Actions agreed and the expected follow-up date.

Write what you know — not what you assume

Describe observable facts rather than interpreting someone's intention or character.

Instead of:

“Jordan clearly doesn't care about being on time and has a bad attitude.”

Write:

“Jordan arrived after the scheduled start time on 3, 7 and 12 September. During our discussion on 12 September, I reviewed the expected start time and asked Jordan whether there were circumstances affecting attendance.”

After the conversation

Before closing your record, check that another person could read it later and understand:

  • What happened?
  • What standard was expected?
  • What did the employee say?
  • What did the manager do?
  • What happens next?
