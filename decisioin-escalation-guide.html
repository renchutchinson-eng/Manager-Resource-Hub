Manager Decision & Escalation Guide

A quick guide to help managers determine when to act, when to seek guidance and when an issue should be escalated.

Before You Decide

Ask yourself five questions:

1. Do I have the facts?

Make sure you understand what happened. Separate confirmed information from assumptions or opinions.

2. Is this within my authority?

Consider whether this is a normal day-to-day management decision or whether approval or specialist guidance is required.

3. Is there an applicable policy or process?

Check the relevant guidance before acting where a formal policy, procedure or requirement may apply.

4. What is the level of risk?

Consider potential impact on employees, safety, customers, compliance, operations and the business.

5. Am I confident about the next step?

If the appropriate action is unclear, seek guidance before making a decision that may be difficult to reverse.

When to Escalate

Pause and seek support where:

  • Formal disciplinary or performance action may be required.
  • There is a significant health or safety concern.
  • Harassment, discrimination or serious misconduct is alleged.
  • A policy or legal requirement may be involved.
  • The issue may create significant employee or business risk.
  • You do not have the authority to make the decision.
  • You are unsure of the appropriate next step.

When You Can Usually Act

  • Routine work allocation and prioritization.
  • Everyday coaching and feedback.
  • Clarifying expectations.
  • Recognizing good performance.
  • Resolving normal operational issues within your authority.

Document the Decision

Where appropriate, record the key facts, the decision made, any guidance received and agreed follow-up actions.

Quick decision check

  • Facts
  • Authority
  • Policy
  • Risk
  • Next step

Not sure?

It is better to seek guidance before taking formal or high-risk action than to try to correct an inappropriate decision afterward.
