Difficult Conversation Guide

A practical guide to help managers prepare for and navigate challenging employee conversations with clarity, respect and confidence.

Before the Conversation

Take a few minutes to prepare before you meet.

  • Be clear about the issue you need to address.
  • Separate facts from assumptions.
  • Identify specific examples where possible.
  • Know what outcome you want from the conversation.
  • Consider what you need to understand from the employee.

During the Conversation

1. Open clearly

Explain why you are having the conversation. Be direct without being confrontational.

2. Describe the facts

Explain what you observed or what occurred using specific, objective information.

3. Listen

Give the employee an opportunity to explain their perspective. Ask questions before reaching conclusions.

4. Clarify expectations

Make sure the employee understands what is expected and why it matters.

5. Agree next steps

Confirm actions, responsibilities and when you will follow up.

Keep the Conversation Constructive

Instead of:

“Your attitude has been terrible lately.”


Try:

“During yesterday's team discussion, you interrupted two colleagues while they were speaking and left before the discussion ended. I'd like to understand what was happening from your perspective.”

If the Conversation Becomes Difficult

  • Stay calm and keep your tone measured.
  • Return to the facts and purpose of the conversation.
  • Acknowledge emotion without arguing about it.
  • Pause the conversation if continuing would not be productive.
  • Seek support where the appropriate next step is unclear.

After the Conversation

  • Document important facts and agreed actions.
  • Follow through on any commitments you made.
  • Monitor progress where appropriate.
  • Hold the agreed follow-up conversation.

Quick preparation check

  • What happened?
  • What evidence or examples do I have?
  • What standard or expectation applies?
  • What do I need to understand?
  • What outcome am I seeking?

When should I get support?

Contact People & Culture before taking formal action, or where the situation involves significant conduct, employee relations, safety, wellbeing or other business risk.
